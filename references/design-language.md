# Developing a distinct character

Use this for a new personification or creative redesign that feels generic. Style-only conversions retain the supplied character concept.

Start with what distinguishes the source in personality, purpose, shape or meaning. Choose a concise interpretation that suggests who this woman is. Let a few recognizable traits support her identity without covering every accessory in the same motif.

Judge the whole first impression: face, presence, silhouette and personal style. For a new source, seek a fresh fashion idea and setting suited to this character. Recoloring the last outfit or repeating its leg styling is a weak substitute for a new interpretation. Keep wardrobe choices open, without a catalog or rotation.

Treat a setting as a compositional suggestion. Choose the place or situation, then draw only enough environmental structure to establish it. Large quiet areas, grouped shapes and lightly indicated secondary objects can carry the atmosphere. Fine background textures and elaborate displays are optional artistic choices, not a quality requirement.

Use references according to their assigned role. A leg-shape photograph informs proportions and contour flow; a named art reference informs drawing and coloring. Carry those qualities into the requested illustration style without inheriting unrelated appearance, garments or photographic surfaces.
